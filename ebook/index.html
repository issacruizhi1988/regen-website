Introduction

Something
Is Missing

Most people who train consistently still feel tight, uncomfortable, or stuck. Not because they are not trying hard enough. Because something deeper has not been addressed.

📸 Replace with your coaching photo

You train. You stretch. You go for treatment when things flare up. And for a while, it helps. But a few weeks later, the same tightness is back. The same discomfort in the same places. You start to wonder: am I just built this way?

You are not. But the way most people approach training is missing a critical layer. The body does not work as a collection of isolated parts. It works as one connected system. And when that system has gaps, no amount of effort fills them.

The Central Idea

"The body is not lacking effort. It is leaking energy, because the system is not connected."

When one part of the body does not do its job, another takes over. This is compensation. Compensations build quietly over months and years, until they show up as tightness, weakness, or pain that keeps returning regardless of what you do.

Something Most People Are Never Told

Your Body Was Never Meant to Be Perfectly Symmetrical

Every human body is built asymmetrically. Your heart sits slightly left of centre. Your liver anchors the right side. Your right diaphragm is larger and more domed. This creates natural, predictable patterns. One shoulder tends to sit higher, one hip rotates more easily, one side carries more tension than the other.

This is not a flaw. It is biology. The body adapts around these asymmetries beautifully, until it runs out of capacity to manage them. Most chronic tightness and recurring pain does not come from the asymmetry itself. It comes from the body no longer being able to cope with it. Understanding this changes how you train and how you stop blaming yourself for problems that were never your fault.

💨
Leak 01
Pressure Leaks
When breathing is not coordinated, the body loses its internal support and creates tension instead of stability, usually in the neck and lower back.
⚡
Leak 02
Force Leaks
When feet, hips, or arms are not connected, force cannot travel efficiently. Joints absorb load that the chain should be sharing across the whole body.
🤹
Leak 03
Coordination Leaks
When movement is not organised, the body uses more effort than necessary. Fatigue builds in the wrong places. Simple things feel harder than they should.

The ReGen Method addresses all three, not by adding more exercises, but by reconnecting the system so everything you already do works better.

"It may not be about doing more. It may be about organising the system better."

Chapter 01

The
Ground

Everything starts here. Before the bar, before the breath, before anything, there is the foot. And most people have completely forgotten how to use it.

📸 Foot tripod diagram or coaching photo

Your feet are the body's first contact point with the world. Every step, every squat, every press begins with how your foot meets the floor. When that contact is clear and organised, force travels cleanly through every joint above. When it is not, every joint above compensates.

The foot has three key contact points: the heel, the ball under the big toe, and the ball under the little toe. We call this the foot tripod. When all three are evenly active, you have a stable, responsive base. When one collapses or grips, the entire system above it shifts.

Why the Big Toe Matters More Than You Think

"The big toe is the final push-off in every step, lunge, and sprint. When it stops doing its job, the knee, hip, and lower back quietly start doing it instead."

01
The Base
Foot Tripod
Heel, big toe ball, little toe ball, all three active and evenly loaded. When this is in place, force distributes cleanly upward through every joint.
02
Upstream Effect
Big Toe and the Posterior Chain
Big toe pressing into the floor activates the entire back line: calves, hamstrings, glutes. A weak big toe means a poorly activated posterior chain, regardless of how hard you try.
03
Upstream Effect
Ankle, Knee, Hip
How your foot loads the floor determines how your knee tracks and how your hip operates. A collapsed arch rotates the knee inward. A gripping foot compresses the joint. Either way, the hip loses efficiency.
04
The Deep Link
Foot and the Pelvic Floor
Through the deep fascial system, foot position and toe strength directly influence pelvic floor activation. This is why foot mechanics matter even for what we traditionally call core problems.
Sound familiar?
  • Knees that cave inward on squats or lunges
  • Hips that feel tight no matter how much you stretch
  • Feet that ache after a long day of standing or walking
  • Feeling unstable during single-leg exercises
  • Lower back switching on before your glutes do

The answer, more often than not, starts at the floor. That is where we begin.

Chapter 02

The
Breath

You do it 20,000 times a day. And most of those times, you are doing it in a way that quietly undermines everything else you do in the gym.

📸 Replace with your coaching photo

Most people breathe into their chest. Shoulders rise. Neck tightens. The lower ribs barely move. This pattern, built through years of stress, prolonged sitting, and screen time, quietly shifts how the entire torso loads and stabilises.

Inside your trunk is a pressure system. Think of it as a cylinder: four walls that work together to support everything inside.

🫁
Top
The Diaphragm
When it contracts well, it descends and creates pressure downward and outward, like a piston driving stability through the trunk before any external load arrives.
Bottom
The Pelvic Floor
Responds to the diaphragm from below. When both work together, they regulate trunk pressure like a two-way valve. No hard bracing or sucking in required.
Sides
The Abdominal Wall
Does not need to be forcefully braced. It responds automatically when the diaphragm and pelvic floor are coordinating correctly, providing effortless support.
Behind
The Posterior Wall
Deep spinal muscles complete the cylinder from behind, contributing to 360 degrees of pressure that supports the spine from the inside out.
The Key Insight

"The diaphragm is not just a breathing muscle. It is your primary stabiliser. When the cylinder works, your spine is supported before any weight is applied."

When breathing breaks down, air floods the chest, ribs flare upward, and the neck takes over. The cylinder collapses. The body loses its internal support and compensates with tension wherever it can find it. This is why some people feel more neck and upper trap tightness the harder they train. They are not getting stronger. They are getting more compressed.

Sound familiar?
  • Shoulders creeping toward your ears when tired or under stress
  • Neck tightness that gets worse with more training, not less
  • Lower back that braces hard instead of feeling supported
  • Feeling winded on exercises that should not exhaust you
  • Upper traps that stay tight regardless of stretching or massage

"The breath is not just oxygen. It is organisation, the body's way of setting itself up before any load arrives."

Chapter 03

The
Line

When the feet are connected and the breath is organised, force needs a pathway. A line that carries it from the ground all the way to the hands, without losing anything along the way.

📸 Posterior chain or deadlift coaching photo

The posterior chain is the body's main highway for load transfer. Heel. Calf. Hamstring. Glute. Lower back. Thoracic spine. Upper back. All the way to the base of the skull. When connected, load is shared across the entire line. No single joint absorbs more than its share.

When disconnected, which is common after years of desk work, load concentrates. Usually in the lower back. Sometimes the neck. Often both. This is why many people feel their back on deadlifts instead of their glutes and hamstrings. The line is not firing as a unit.

The Upper Anchor

"The shoulder blade is not just part of the shoulder. It is the bridge between your arms and your trunk. When it moves freely, force transfers cleanly. When it is stuck, everything above compensates."

The scapula must be able to glide, tilt, and rotate freely along the rib cage for pressing and pulling to work correctly. And here is something most people never hear: how you grip changes how your shoulder blade moves. The way your knuckles contact a bar directly affects the muscle that wraps your scapula around your rib cage. A small grip adjustment can change how a pressing exercise feels, immediately.

01
The Launch
Heel, Hamstring, Glute
When the foot tripod is active and the big toe engaged, tension builds up the back line automatically. The glute fires because the chain below it is connected, not because you thought about it.
02
The Cross
Glute, Thoracolumbar Fascia, Lat
Force crosses the midline through a thick sheet of connective tissue linking the glute to the opposite lat. This cross-body connection makes heavy movements feel whole, stable, and powerful.
03
The Anchor
Rib Cage, Scapula, Arm
The shoulder blade wraps around the rib cage. For it to move well, the ribs must be stacked over the pelvis, not flared. Rib position is scapula health. Scapula health is shoulder health.
04
The Detail
Knuckles, Serratus, Scapula
Pressing through all knuckles, especially the pinky side, activates the muscle responsible for wrapping the scapula around the rib cage. Grip is never just grip.
Sound familiar?
  • Lower back that switches on during deadlifts instead of your glutes
  • Shoulders that shrug during pulling exercises
  • Upper traps that stay tight no matter what you do
  • Pressing that lives in the shoulder, not the chest and upper back
  • Back that tightens during simple tasks like carrying bags

Chapter 04

The
Signal

Above the feet, the breath, and the line sits the most important layer, the one that controls every other layer. And the one most training programmes completely ignore.

📸 Movement or coaching photo

Your nervous system has one primary job: keep you safe. To do that, it reads signals from every joint, muscle, and sense organ, thousands of inputs per second, and decides how much movement, strength, and flexibility to allow at any moment.

If it senses something uncertain, an unstable foot, a compressed rib cage, a neck that has lost its position, it protects you. It limits your range of motion. It dials down strength. It creates tension as a warning. The brain is not being difficult. It is being logical.

The Key Insight

"Your brain is not limiting you because something is broken. It is limiting you because the information it is receiving is not good enough. Better input equals more output."

🦴
Input 01
Joint Position
When foot, knee, hip, and spine are stacked well, the brain receives clear positional data and allows free, full movement without restriction.
💆
Input 02
Head & Neck
Your inner ear tells the brain where your head is in space. Neck mechanics and head position directly influence what the rest of the body can access.
💨
Input 03
Internal Pressure
Organised breathing and good trunk pressure communicate safety from the inside out, before external load even arrives. The cylinder is a signal, not just a stabiliser.
👁️
Input 04
Vision
Where your eyes point affects neck tension and the brain's sense of orientation. Eye position is rarely trained, but it is powerfully influential on everything below it.

When you address all four inputs, feet, breath, mechanics, and head position, the output changes. Strength increases. Range opens. Chronic tension that persisted for years can release in a single session. Not because something was fixed. Because the brain was given a reason to let go.

Sound familiar?
  • Strength that varies significantly from day to day for no obvious reason
  • Range of motion that disappears under load even though you feel flexible
  • Chronic tension that returns no matter what treatment you try
  • Movements that feel blocked even when nothing is structurally wrong
  • Performance that drops when stressed or not sleeping well

"You are not broken. Your nervous system is making a very reasonable decision, based on information that can be changed."

Chapter 05

The
Work

The same system runs through every rep you do and every hour you spend outside the gym. Here is how to use it.

Before You Train — A Reset, Not a Warm-Up

Most people walk into the gym and jump straight into warming up: some light cardio, a few stretches, maybe a foam roll. This is not wrong. But it misses something important.

A warm-up prepares the body for effort. A reset prepares the body for position.

Why This Matters

Because of how the body naturally compensates, and the asymmetric patterns we all carry from daily life, the body often arrives at the gym in a slightly off-balance state. One side compressed, the other elongated. One hip sitting slightly forward, one behind. The rib cage stacked in a way that favours one direction of rotation.

Loading that position, even with technically correct form, reinforces it over time. A reset is different. It is 1 to 2 short, targeted exercises designed to bring the body closer to a neutral, balanced starting position before any load is applied. Not a random stretch. Not a generic mobility drill. A specific bias that counters your natural tendency and allows the muscles and joints to work from a better starting point.

🦵🏼
Reset Type 01
Feet and Pelvis
Before loading any pattern, attention goes to two foundational positions: how the feet contact the floor, and where the pelvis sits. Establishing an active foot tripod and a neutral pelvic position sets the base from which every muscle and joint above can operate properly. This is where compensation most often starts, and where the reset most often begins.
😮‍💨
Reset Type 02
A Breathing Position
A short breathing-based drill that encourages both sides of the rib cage to move evenly and the pelvis to find a more neutral orientation. Usually 5 to 8 breaths. Often surprisingly effective on the first set. This is not deep breathing for relaxation. It is positional breathing to restore the internal pressure system before any loading begins.

The specific reset depends on your assessment: your posture, your history, your natural compensation pattern. This is something we identify in the first session. But the principle is always the same: organise the system first, then load it.

How a Session Is Built

We do not select exercises based on muscle groups alone. Every session is built in deliberate layers, each one building on the one before it.

01
Reset
1 to 2 exercises to bring the body into a more balanced starting position. Short, targeted, specific to your pattern. This is the layer most programmes skip entirely.
02
Position and Pattern
The first working exercises are chosen to strengthen the body in the position the reset established. The goal is to lock in and reinforce better alignment while the nervous system still remembers it.
03
Load and Build
The main strength work: squat, hinge, press, pull, loaded progressively. The body is now in a better position to absorb and distribute that load without defaulting to its old compensation pattern.
04
Respect and Adapt
The sequence and combination of exercises is chosen to work with, and gradually shift, your body's natural compensation tendencies. Over time, this changes what your body considers its default resting position.

The result: strength built on top of organisation. Not just bigger muscles reinforcing the same old pattern.

The Four Patterns
A Note on Breathing and Lifting
For most training
Exhale on effort. This keeps the trunk connected, prevents the accidental breath hold, and helps the nervous system stay calm under load. The exhalation itself creates stability. It is not a loss of pressure.
For heavy loads
Inhale, set the cylinder intentionally, then move. This is a deliberate pressure strategy, not the same as holding your breath out of tension or fear. The key word is always intentional.
Watch the Method in Action
🏋️The SquatFull Chain
Common pattern: lower back overworks as the foot tripod collapses and the hips lose their anchor to the ground.
What Is Actually Happening
  • Foot tripod must be alive before any load reaches the spine
  • Big toe into the floor primes the entire posterior chain
  • For moderate loads, exhale before descent to organise pressure
  • For heavy loads, inhale, set the cylinder, then descend
  • Rib position determines how deep you can go safely
Cues That Actually Work
  • Feel all three tripod points before you move
  • Ribs down, not chest up. Close the space
  • Push through the big toe, not just the heel
  • Eyes level, chin slightly in throughout
  • Depth comes from the hips, not from rounding the lower back
🔩The Hinge (Deadlift / RDL)Posterior Chain
Common pattern: lower back bends instead of the hips hinging, because the posterior chain is not loaded from the feet upward.
What Is Actually Happening
  • The chain must be loaded from the floor up before the pull begins
  • Hips are the hinge. The lower back should not change shape
  • For heavy pulls, inhale and set pressure before the bar leaves the floor
  • Exhale at the top once the movement is complete
  • The bar moves because the chain moves, not the arms or back
Cues That Actually Work
  • Before pulling, feel tension build from feet up through hamstrings
  • Push the floor away. The bar follows as a result
  • Shoulder blades heavy, toward your back pockets
  • Feeling it in your lower back? Raise your hips slightly
  • The glutes fire at the top. If not, the chain below is not loaded
💪The Press (Push-up / Bench / Overhead)Scapula + Rib Cage
Common pattern: shoulder absorbs the load because the scapula cannot move freely along a rib cage that is flared or stiff.
What Is Actually Happening
  • Knuckle pressure directly affects how the shoulder blade moves
  • The scapula must wrap the rib cage as you press, not be pinned back
  • Rib position determines whether the scapula has room to move
  • For most pressing, exhale as you push to stay connected
  • For maximal pressing, brief pressure hold, then exhale at lockout
Cues That Actually Work
  • Press through all knuckles evenly, especially the pinky side
  • Let the shoulder blade move forward and around the rib cage
  • Ribs closed, not flaring toward the ceiling
  • Push the ground or bar away. Feel the upper back engage
  • Neck long throughout. Do not let it shorten under load
🚣The Pull (Row / Pull-up / Cable)Upper Back + Scapula
Common pattern: arms and upper traps do the work because the scapula is not moving and breath is not set before the pull begins.
What Is Actually Happening
  • Pulling should be felt in the mid-back and lats, not the arms
  • If the scapula is not moving, the biceps absorb everything
  • If breath is not set, neck and traps take over the pull
  • The scapula must retract and depress, not just shrug backward
  • Head position affects how much the upper back can contribute
Cues That Actually Work
  • Elbow to back pocket, not pulling the weight toward you
  • Feel the shoulder blade slide toward the spine and downward
  • Shoulders stay away from ears throughout the full range
  • Exhale on the effort. Stay connected through the trunk
  • Feeling it in your neck? Reduce load and reset the breath first
Beyond the Gym

You spend one hour in the gym. The other twenty-three hours are where the system is either reinforced or slowly undone. For most desk workers, the hours outside training are where the most damage accumulates and where the most gains are lost.

🛡️
The Gym as Armour
When you build strength through organised patterns, your body becomes more resilient to the accumulated stress of daily life. The sitting, the phone, the carrying matter less when the system is strong. Training is not separate from daily life. It is the reason daily life hurts less.
The stronger and better-organised your body, the more daily stress it can absorb without breaking down.
💻
At Your Desk
A forward head position sends a low-level threat signal to the nervous system for as long as you hold it. Eight hours of screen time is eight hours of compressed neck, elevated upper traps, and restricted rib cage.
Try: sit bones down, ribs over pelvis, screen at eye level. Breathe into the lower ribs, not the chest. Set a reminder every 45 minutes.
🚶
Walking
Walking is the most underrated training tool most people ignore. Every step is an opportunity to load the foot tripod, activate the posterior chain, and reinforce a rhythmic breathing pattern.
Try: feel the heel land, roll through the foot, push off the big toe. Let the arms swing naturally. Breathe through the nose.
👜
Carrying Bags
A single-shoulder bag shifts the entire system. One shoulder elevates, the spine rotates, the hip hikes. Over time this reinforces the asymmetry patterns that drive recurring pain.
Try: alternate sides regularly or use a backpack. Notice which side always feels more loaded. That is your dominant compensation pattern.
📱
On Your Phone
Looking down at a screen drops your head forward and multiplies the effective load on the neck, sending a continuous compression signal through the entire upper body hundreds of times a day.
Try: raise the screen to eye level. Take a full exhale every 10 minutes. Let the upper traps drop before you look back down.
😴
Sleep Position
How you sleep sets the pattern your body wakes up in. Stomach sleeping compresses the neck and extends the lower back. Side sleeping with a collapsed hip loads one side consistently night after night.
Try: side sleeping with a pillow between the knees to keep the hips neutral. Let the spine decompress overnight rather than reinforce tension.

"Every rep is a conversation between your nervous system and your environment. So is every hour at your desk."
